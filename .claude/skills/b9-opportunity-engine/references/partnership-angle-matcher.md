# E. Partnership Architect (angle matcher)

Build **two to five organization-specific partnership structures** for each
prospect, then LEAD THE EMAIL WITH ONE. The extra structures are not filler
for the email — they are what Neil uses on the reply, and they go into the
ledger as `opportunity_type`.

The email carries one strong lead idea, optionally with two or three
concise supporting options. Never a menu of ten. The reader should think:
"This actually makes sense for us."

## The rejection test (module J applies it)

If a partnership concept could be pasted unchanged into an email to ten
unrelated businesses, it is not a concept — it is a template. Rewrite it
around this organization's real audience, calendar, services and goals, or
reject the prospect.

Concretely: "we could do a staff night" is generic. "Your fabrication floor
finishes shifts at odd hours and our bays are open at all of them, and the
shops you supply are the group bookings we already take" is specific.

## Angle menu (choose one per business)

- feature them as a partner on the facility TVs (a paid advertising
  placement Back Nine sells — never imply it's free)
- sponsor a tournament / league / prize / challenge hole
- staff night / team-building event / staff appreciation
- client appreciation event
- customer reward (their customers earn Back Nine perks)
- co-branded local campaign
- referral relationship (two-way customer flow)
- private golf night / private event hosting
- gift experience (their gift cards + ours, holiday bundles)
- member perk (their offer to our members, ours to theirs)
- sector hooks: health/wellness (mobility, recovery, screening events),
  hospitality (guest activity, weather-day option), real estate (closing
  gifts, client events), automotive (customer-appreciation, launch nights),
  tourism (itinerary stop, cross-referral), retail (cross-promo, prize
  partner), trades (crew wind-ups, referral-network nights), professional
  services (client entertainment, post-season parties)

## Matching heuristics

- Who walks through their door, and would those people enjoy Back Nine?
- Who works there, and do they need a team event?
- What do they sell that Back Nine's high-end clientele buys?
- What season are they slow — can Back Nine fill it (or vice versa)?
- What do they celebrate (closings, launches, season ends) that needs a venue?

Pick the single best answer and write the email toward it.
