# Back Nine Golf Vernon — Brand, Visual Standard & Defaults

Default client for most content. A premium, modern, private, technologically advanced, social,
welcoming, year-round, confident, fun-without-being-childish 24/7 indoor golf facility in Vernon,
British Columbia — focused on golf, experience, technology, lifestyle, and community.

## Never make Back Nine look

cheap · cluttered · generic · arcade-like · childish · overly neon · unrealistic · like a
game-engine environment.

## Default scene ingredients

golfers · simulator bays · Full Swing simulator technology · golf clubs · **one golf ball** ·
golf bags · premium lounge areas · Back Nine Vernon branding · dark modern architecture ·
realistic green turf · large simulator screens · glass doors · brushed metal details ·
controlled architectural lighting.

## Default assumptions when the user gives only a brief concept

- Characters are golfers.
- Location is Back Nine Vernon — a premium simulator bay or connected facility area.
- Props: golf clubs, **one golf ball**, a golf bag, gloves, touchscreens, simulator screens,
  Back Nine branding.
- Mood: premium, cinematic, modern, confident, authentic.
- **Do not** introduce additional golf balls, unrelated props, food, drinks, random spectators,
  or unnecessary background people/activity unless the user requests them.

## Default visual standard (unless the user specifies otherwise)

4K photorealism · premium commercial cinematography · realistic skin/material texture · realistic
body movement · accurate anatomy · realistic hands/fingers · realistic golf equipment · physically
accurate ball movement · natural motion blur · fine film grain · believable depth of field ·
physically motivated lighting · correct contact shadows · accurate reflections · realistic weight
and inertia · stable identity/wardrobe/props/location/branding · no game-engine or synthetic CGI
look. Nothing warped, stretched, melted, duplicated, disconnected, floating, fused, physically
impossible, or unstable between frames.

## Brand palette

- Back Nine green — `#96CB39`
- deep navy — `#10171F`
- soft white — `#F2F2F2`
- charcoal — `#333333`
- accent gold — `#D4AF57`
- accent blue — `#39B8DE`
- accent peach-copper — `#F0A57A`

Accent colours must be tied to a physical light source, a screen, a reflection, a material, or
official branding / architectural lighting. **Do not flood the scene with colour. No cheap neon
overload.**

## Color grade (default balance)

Deep navy and charcoal dominate · soft white defines neutral highlights · Back Nine green appears
as a controlled accent · simulator-screen light gives realistic cool illumination · warm practical
lights may add contrast · skin tones stay natural · turf stays realistic · club reflections stay
metallic and accurate · the single ball stays clearly readable without glowing unnaturally. No
flat overlays.

## Brand integration

Integrate the brand naturally through architecture, bay dividers, wall signage, doors, screens,
apparel, golf bags, environmental colour, reflected light, final compositions, and controlled CTA
frames. Branding should feel premium and believable, never pasted on. Preserve exact logo
proportions, official colours, and typography; prevent misspellings and logo mutation; use
supplied logo assets whenever possible — the official logo must never be improvised incorrectly.

## Audio

When audio is requested, separate dialogue · ambience · Foley · mechanical sound · environmental
sound · music, and state whether music is allowed. When the user requests no music, write:

```
NO MUSIC.
NO SCORE.
NO SOUNDTRACK.
DIEGETIC AUDIO ONLY.
```

Dialogue: brief, natural, easy to sync, appropriate for the clip length, written exactly as spoken.
If a speaker is offscreen, state they remain offscreen and never enter the frame.

Relevant golf audio: club movement · grip movement · footsteps on turf · tee contact · **one**
clean club-to-ball impact · **one** ball striking the simulator screen · **one** ball rolling ·
**one** ball rebounding. Do not create multiple ball-impact sounds unless the story clearly shows
several separate shots at different times using the same single ball.

## Performance direction

Subtle, believable behaviour: breathing · posture changes · shoulder tension · jaw release · eye
movement · hand/grip pressure · weight transfer · micro-expressions · hesitation · anticipation ·
concentration · controlled reactions · relaxed confidence. Avoid exaggerated acting unless
requested. Protect natural eyes, accurate blinking, realistic teeth, consistent facial identity,
natural skin texture, lip sync, stable hair/wardrobe, natural hand movement, anatomically correct
fingers.
