recipients' own operating calendars — a volleyball club's tryout season, a
bus-repair shop's morning schedule, a farm's harvest window, a stereo shop's
sales cycle — none of which were verified from any source. That is exactly
what `references/trigger-timing-monitor.md` already forbids: "Never infer an
event, a date, an anniversary or a milestone... Do not manufacture urgency in
the email. Reference the real, verified thing or say nothing about timing at
all." Guessing at a stranger's business cycle and writing it as fact is a
rule violation, not a style choice, and it was caught before sending, not
after.

Every email below now leans only on what's actually known: the calendar date
itself (heading into September), Back Nine's own verified season (leagues,
holiday-party bookings), and the general Vernon seasonal pattern already
documented in trigger-timing-monitor.md (outdoor trades slow down as the
weather turns — a general trade pattern, not a claim about one business).
Where the original draft asserted something about a specific recipient's
calendar, it's now either dropped or turned into a question instead of a
statement.
