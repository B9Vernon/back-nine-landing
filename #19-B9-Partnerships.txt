Run 8 delivered 200 businesses of which FOUR had an email address. Run 11
delivered 250 of which FOUR had an email address. Those runs worked because
they delivered the phone numbers and web forms that most local businesses
actually publish.

At run 12 a rule was introduced: every To: line must be a real email address.
Every run since has been 100% email and none has broken twenty. The engine
never got worse at finding businesses. It got a rule that throws away roughly
95% of what it finds, and then each run honestly reported the small number
that survived.

The rule was answering a real problem: a bare contact-page URL sitting in the
recipient field is useless when you are pasting into Gmail. The fix for that
is to LABEL the channel, not to discard the business.

WHAT CHANGED, PERMANENTLY

  SKILL.md step 3        "Email found → keep, no email → drop" is withdrawn.
                         Keep every business with any verified way to reach
                         it. The recipient line now declares the channel.
  b9lib.contact_channel  New. A recipient line is valid as a bare email, as
                         "FORM <url>", or as "PHONE <number>". An unlabelled
                         contact-page URL is still rejected — that was the
                         real defect.
  verify_deliverable.py  Now checks every recipient line declares a usable
                         channel and prints the mix. --email-only still
                         exists but is OPT-IN, and its help says what it
                         costs.
  fit_score.py           contact_quality was scoring the CHANNEL, so a local
                         shop's main phone line scored below a generic info@
                         at a national chain. It now scores how close the
                         contact gets you to a decision, channel-neutral: a
                         business's main line and a role inbox both score 6.
