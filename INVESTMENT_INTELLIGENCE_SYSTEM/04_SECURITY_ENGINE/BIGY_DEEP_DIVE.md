## THE DECIDING NUMBER
"Total return" ALREADY INCLUDES every distribution paid. A total return of ~-15% therefore means:
after collecting all those big cheques, the position is still down ~15%. The distributions are
not additive to the loss — they are already counted in it, and the NAV fell by more than the
income paid. That is capital consumption, verified — not asserted.

## THE CONTROLLED EXPERIMENT (investor's own account)
BIGY and HYLD are the SAME underlying trade: US large-cap equity + covered call + ~1.25-1.33x leverage.
- HYLD: pays 12.04%. Investor's result: **+61.40%**
- BIGY: pays ~40%+ (annualizing $0.3125 x 24). Investor's result: **-7.25% on price, ~-15% total return**
Same exposure, same wrapper type, same investor, same period. The variable that differs is the
PAYOUT RATE. The fund distributing a rate its portfolio can actually earn compounded; the fund
distributing far beyond what it earns paid the difference out of unitholder capital.
This is the cleanest available evidence that the payout rate — not the underlying — is the problem.
